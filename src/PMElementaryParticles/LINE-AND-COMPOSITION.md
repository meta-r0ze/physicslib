## 1. The line problem: what closes the receiver ring

**Reframing.** Task B5 looked for a lattice line through the vacuum vector
`+2e₀`. That was the wrong question. The vacuum vector is the background
*value* at every site, not the direction of the line. `RingReceiver` says
what the ring is: "A periodic identification closes the encoded root line."
The receiver runs on the encoded root line (`LocalController` on `ℤ`,
`OpenHandoff.address_step` along a primitive root), and the ring is that line
closed periodically. The open question is what supplies the closure.

**Proposal: the clock closes it.** The clock's states are the discriminant
group of the E8 clock plane `ℤcu + ℤcv` (`PMSubstrate.ClockPlane`:
`clock_glue`, `w ↦ w₁ − 2w₂ mod 43`). A lattice point reads the clock by
pairing with the plane: `phase(z) = (⟨z,cu⟩ − 2⟨z,cv⟩) mod 43`.

`tools/clock_ring_check.py` finds:

| Count | Value |
| --- | --- |
| E8 roots | 240 |
| roots that change the clock reading | 218 |
| roots that leave it unchanged | 22 (20 orthogonal to the clock plane, plus `±cu`) |
| roots that advance it by exactly `+1` | 22 |
| roots that advance it by exactly `−1` | 22, the negatives of the `+1` roots |
| split of the `+1` roots by `(⟨r,cu⟩, ⟨r,cv⟩)` | 11 with `(1,0)`, 11 with `(−1,−1)` |

A walk that makes one root event per tick, each changing the reading by `±1`,
lives on `ℤ/43`. Because 43 is prime, the closure has exactly 43 sites. The
ring size is the clock period, `n = Δ`. It is not a free choice, and it is
not 3.

**The two streams.** The live stream steps by `+1` roots and the record stream
by `−1` roots. They move in opposite directions, as in `RingReceiver`. An
exchange swaps a packet between them, which reverses its direction.

**Consequences, if this holds:**

- The 3-ring numbers are replaced by 43-ring numbers. One firing site gives
  exactly one registration per period (`tools/bit_ring_rate_check.py`,
  `n = 43`). This is no longer lap arithmetic: the lap is the clock period.
- The Toll floor (at least one registration per period) is met exactly by one
  firing site per period. Margin still excludes trapping between adjacent
  firing sites.
- A ring site is a clock phase. Requests retained per site are requests per
  phase slot. This matches `ClockedRootTrials`, which fires at phases 0 and 1.

**Checks still needed:**

1. *Step weights* (`tools/clock_stabilizer_orbits.py`; numerical, not proved).
   The pointwise stabilizer of the clock plane is generated by reflections in
   the 20 roots orthogonal to it. They form an `A₄` system (rank 4), and the group is
   `W(A₄) ≅ S₅`, of order 120. The roots orthogonal to that `A₄` form a second
   `A₄` that contains `cu`. So the clock plane picks out `E₈ ⊃ A₄ × A₄`: one
   `A₄` holds the clock and the other is its stabilizer.
   The 22 one-tick roots fall into four orbits:

   | Projection class | Orbits |
   | --- | --- |
   | `(1,0)` | one fixed root plus an orbit of 10 |
   | `(−1,−1)` | one fixed root plus an orbit of 10 |

   The two fixed roots lie in the clock's `A₄`. The 10-orbits mix the two `A₄`
   systems. The classes are paired: `r ↦ r − cu` maps class `(1,0)` onto class
   `(−1,−1)` (`cu` is clock-silent and fixed by the stabilizer).
   Closure therefore fixes equal weights inside each orbit. No data enters; the
   `5` of `S₅` is the substrate's, not an input.

   **Proved in the certified aligned placement**
   (`PMKAutomaton/ClockTickWeights.lean`, with check 2). The receiver's forward
   tick steps are the D4's six clock-axis roots that read `+1`
   (`forwardRoots_length`), and the backward steps are the six at `-1`
   (`backwardRoots_length`). Every slice root is clock-silent and a reflection
   in it fixes the clock reading (`clockReading_reflect`). The slice
   reflections preserve the forward and backward sets (`forward_closed`,
   `backward_closed`) and act transitively on each set (`forward_one_orbit`,
   `backward_one_orbit`; two reflections suffice).
   Therefore the six forward steps form one orbit of substrate symmetries that
   fix the clock, and the six backward steps form another. By Closure — a
   weight defined inside the substrate is invariant under the substrate's
   symmetries that fix the clock — the six forward steps carry equal weight, and
   so do the six backward steps. Closure itself is the stated premise, as in
   `SliceHop`. No open weight remains once Closure is granted.
2. *Consistency with `AxisHop` / `SliceHop`* (`tools/clock_slice_alignment.py`).
   `SliceHop` uses a D4 sector with coordinate 3 as its clock axis. Hops in
   the slice (clock coordinate 0) stay within a tick, and the roots through
   the clock axis move the clock coordinate by `±1`. The library never placed
   that D4 relative to the E8 clock plane. With the current `sectorBasis` the
   two disagree: slice roots change `clockReading` by 0 or `±6`.

   **There are placements where they agree exactly.** Search over every D4
   whose slice D3 lies in the clock plane's stabilizer `A₄` (numerical; the
   row counts and the ten-placement robustness are not Lean theorems):

   | Readings of the 12 clock-axis roots | Labelled placements |
   | --- | --- |
   | `±1` (6 each) | 960 |
   | `±2`, `±4`, `±8` | 480 each |

   In the `±1` class:
   - every slice root is orthogonal to the clock plane, so spatial hops are
     clock-silent, as `SliceHop` says;
   - every clock-axis root is a one-tick root, so `e₀ ± e₃` reads `±1`. That
     matches `SliceHop`'s "one clock event forward, one backward" for the two
     clock-routed hop words;
   - the clock-axis step `2e₃` reads `+2`: two ticks per axis step, matching
     `AxisHop`'s two root events;
   - the 6 forward tick roots all lie in one projection class and in its
     10-orbit, never in the fixed roots. The two classes split the placements
     480/480;
   - the orthogonal complement is again a D4, so `E₈ = D₄ ⊕ D₄` still holds.

   So the two pictures are consistent on one condition: **the spacetime D4 is
   placed so that one step along its clock axis is one E8 clock tick.** The
   current `sectorBasis` does not satisfy this. It should be replaced by an
   aligned placement, or the alignment stated as the named condition it is.
   With alignment, the receiver's tick steps are the D4 clock-axis roots,
   6 forward and 6 back. That also narrows the open step-weight question in
   check 1.
3. *Clock-silent roots.* The 22 roots with phase 0 do not move a packet around
   the ring. Say what they do: internal or transverse motion.

## 2. The composition problem: the nested rules on the machine

The three rules (`PM-3-Nested/paper/2_nested_persistence.tex`), stated on the
automaton:

- **Capacity Partitioning.** Proved in Lean. The general
  rule is `PersistenceMechanics.CapacityPartitioning`: `global_budget`,
  `count_lower_bound`, and `bounded_backlog_zero_drift` / `service_balance` (a
  bounded queue over a repeating cycle has zero drift). The machine instance is
  `PMElementaryParticles.CapacityLedger`. One ring offers `Δ = 43` exchange
  opportunities per period. If every protocol meets the Toll floor, at most 43
  protocols share a ring (`ring_protocol_bound`). With a Margin reserve `R` kept
  free, at most `43 − R` do (`ring_protocol_bound_reserve`).
- **Reversible Encoding.** The joint encoding must be injective. The paper's
  relational form: values relative to a reference `x_o`, together with the
  reference value itself, because "dropping the reference value merges
  distinct constant configurations."
- **Impedance Matching.** Proved in Lean. The general
  rule is `PersistenceMechanics.ImpedanceMatching`: `ledger`,
  `admissible_iff_transmission`, `zero_reserve_matched`, `realize`. The machine
  instance is `PMElementaryParticles.ImpedanceLedger`. There the certified
  converter's flip probability `flag x = sin²(x/2)` is the reflected fraction
  `r²`, and its pass probability is the transmission `T`
  (`converter_is_interface`, `converter_ledger`). The equivalent interface has
  source `1 − s` and load `1 + s`, with `s = |sin(x/2)|`. The exact record bit
  `x = π` is the total-reflection limit. Over a period, reflected plus
  transmitted load is the number of encounters (`period_ledger`). With zero
  reserve nothing registers (`zero_reserve_no_registration`). So a protocol
  that meets the Toll floor needs a positive reserve (`toll_needs_reserve`):
  Rule 3 on the machine is the converter's ledger plus the Margin reserve.

**A composite state.** `k` protocols on one 43-ring, sharing the clock and the
vacuum (`+2e₀`, task B3), each with its own packet. The firing pattern is
joint, and Toll adds over packets (`record_step_identity` per ring).

**The colour question: resolved, and the earlier hypothesis is withdrawn.**
Reversible Encoding is proved in Lean: the general rule
`PersistenceMechanics.ReversibleEncoding` and its instance
`PMElementaryParticles.RecordEncoding`.

- The record `labelRecord` merges labels: six per colour record class and two per
  lepton class (`record_fibers`). So the record alone is not reversible
  (`record_not_injective`).
- The packet's own channel code is reversible metadata
  (`packet_encoding_injective`). Rule 2 holds inside the packet, so no external
  colour store is needed. Any reversible metadata has at least six states
  (`metadata_lower_bound`).
- The record comparator is colour-blind: every record Toll is invariant under
  colour permutation (`bitToll_colour_blind`).

Tasks B3 and B4 treated the colour reference record as a set of three basis vectors
`{e₁, e₂, e₃}`, and concluded that one site cannot hold it. That reading was wrong.
The record comparison is bit-level (projective). It compares the lepton-axis bit and
the sign bit, not individual channels. A single state with no lepton-axis component
and positive colour components has exactly the colour reference record. The
colour-symmetric state `(0,1,1,1)` is one (`colourSymmetric_lepton_axis`,
`colourSymmetric_positive`), and it is fixed by every colour permutation
(`colourSymmetric_invariant`).

Consequences:

- **The colour reference does not force composites.** Colour protocols meet Map and
  the other pillars alone at the record level, as in the survivor table. Whatever
  makes colour persist only in combination, if anything does, is not the reference
  or Reversible Encoding. It has to come from somewhere else, such as colour-flux
  dynamics in Hadrons.
- **No partial comparison angle.** The overlap `1/√3` (angle `arccos(1/√3)`) would
  appear only for a comparator that resolves individual channels
  (`colour_axis_overlap`). No certified comparator does, so it does not enter the
  record Toll. It is withdrawn as a source of cost differences.
- **The vacuum question reopens.** Both references are single-site states: `+e₀` for
  lepton, the colour-symmetric state for colour. The shared-vacuum tables of task P4
  apply again. In the colour vacuum up fails Map; in the lepton vacuum the neutrino
  does.

## 3. Generations under the clock

`tools/generation_clock_signature.py` (exploration, no data).

With the spacetime D4 aligned to the clock plane, `E₈` splits as
`D₄(spacetime) ⊕ D₄(internal)` plus three glue cosets of 64 roots each. The glue
classes are the generations: the three nontrivial `D₄` glue classes, related by
triality (`PMKAutomaton/GENERATIONS.md`). Exact triality would make the three cosets
identical. The aligned clock does not. The numerical search over the 10 distinct
aligned placements found the same signatures. For the certified placement in
`PMKAutomaton.ClockAlignment` the table is proved by `internalRoots_length`,
`spacetimeRoots_length`, `clockAxisRoots_counts`, `vectorRoots_counts`,
`spinorRoots_counts` and `conjugateSpinorRoots_counts`:

| Coset (relative to the spacetime frame) | one-tick `+1` | one-tick `−1` | clock-silent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| vector | 7 | 7 | 2 |
| spinor | 4 | 4 | 0 |
| conjugate spinor | 4 | 4 | 8 |
| (spacetime `D₄`) | 6 | 6 | 12 |
| (internal `D₄`) | 1 | 1 | 0 |

The totals match the clock counts: 22 one-tick roots each way and 22 silent
(`plus_total`, `minus_total`, `silent_total`). The ten-placement invariance of
the table is numerical, not proved. The vector coset is identified by its
clock-axis components (0 or `±1`; the spinor cosets have `±½`); the `±cu`
membership is `cu_key` and `neg_cu_key`.

**What this establishes.** The clock breaks triality completely. All three
generations are distinguishable by the steps available to them: one generation has
the most one-tick steps (7), and the other two share 4 but differ in clock-silent
steps (0 against 8). This is the first difference between generations derived from
E8 alone.

**Matching the cosets to the library's glue address `g`.** In the library a
generation's glue address `g` omits its own class: its walls are the classes
`g ± 1` (`ControlledGluedRegister.wallClass`). Class 0 is the reference class.
`g = 0` is the direct address: its walls avoid the reference, and it supplies the
unsuppressed row (`HeavyFlipControl.direct_iff`). `g = 1, 2` need the reference
node, and their link weights are equal (`GENERATIONS.md`). So generation `g`
corresponds to the coset of its own class, and the question is which coset is the
reference class 0.

Numerically, in every aligned placement **the vector coset carries the clock**.
Its two clock-silent roots are exactly `±cu`, the clock-plane root, and it
contains 8 roots of the clock's own `A₄`; the spinor cosets contain no root of
the clock's `A₄`. In the certified placement, `cu_key` and `neg_cu_key` prove
that `±cu` lie in the vector coset; the exact two-silent-root statement and the
`A₄` count of 8 remain numerical. The coset singled out by the spacetime frame
and the coset that carries the clock are the same coset in every searched
placement.

Taking the reference class to be the coset that carries the clock (a named
identification: the reference is clock-driven, `CarrierReferenceCycle`, but no
theorem places it in a coset), the matching is:

| Glue address | Library role | Coset | one-tick `±1` | silent |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `g = 0` | direct, unsuppressed row | vector (carries `±cu`) | 7 | 2 |
| `g = 1, 2` | reference node, equal link weight | spinor | 4 | 0 |
| | | conjugate spinor | 4 | 8 |

Which of `g = 1, 2` is the 0-silent coset depends on the orientation of
`GluedRegister.triality` (`c ↦ c + 1`), which is not fixed here.

**Two consistency checks with the existing library.**

- The library says the two restoring addresses have equal link weight. The clock
  gives them equal one-tick step counts (4 and 4). That agrees.
- The library says the middle-to-lightest split is open. The clock separates the
  two restoring cosets only by clock-silent steps (0 against 8). So silent steps
  are the one E8 difference available for that split.

**A constraint on the dynamics rule.** Under this matching, the clock-silent count
cannot be the overall mass ordering. The heavy direct generation (`g = 0`) has 2
silent steps, between the two light ones (0 and 8). The one-tick count is what
separates `g = 0` (7) from `g = 1, 2` (4). So a rule for cost per period must use
the one-tick count for the heavy-to-light step and can use silent steps only to
split the two light generations. It has not been chosen, and nothing here is
compared with the tier ledger.
