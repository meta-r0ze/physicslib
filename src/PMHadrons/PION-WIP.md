# Pion dressing, decay thresholds, and vacuum energy (WIP)

A free pion has baryon number zero and lies below the single-nucleon mass. A real Δ can decay to a nucleon plus a pion because its excitation energy exceeds the pion threshold. For an isolated proton, the neutron plus charged pion channel is closed to real emission. Its spin and charge structure can still enter the stationary proton through the pion interaction.

In the pillar picture, Margin locates the emission threshold. The threshold alone gives no mixing amplitude. In static second-order perturbation theory a higher intermediate channel with gap ΔE and matrix element V contributes −|V|²/ΔE to the core energy. The axial charge measures spin–isospin response. A percentage change in that response carries no corresponding energy requirement.

### Open cosmological question

The fixed-volume calculation already permits pion dressing on existing sites. A separate hypothesis is that expansion creates usable capacity and transfers energy into local persistent excitations. Extra capacity counts available states. To make an energy prediction, specify a substrate energy observable and an update that shows what loses energy when an excitation gains it. Compare two histories with identical initial local states and different node-creation rates. Derive the difference in excitation energy before comparing with particle masses or the axial charge. The existing [capacity theorem](../PMCosmology/InformationCapacity.lean) supplies the counting constraint.
